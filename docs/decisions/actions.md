# Actions and the Animate mode

Decisions worth knowing about actions and the Animate mode, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` on 28 October 2026 so they load only when needed. Keep each entry up to date when its code changes.

- **Action value rows** are `StackedRow`s (name above, fields the full width, a starting value's clear button beside the name), so four-digit values show. To or By is chosen from the end row's name (`StackedRow(menu:)`, a `MenuLabel`), not a segment row. The end row's name line carries a swap button in the button column (`StackedRow(swap:)`, `SwapButton`, the user's request on 29 September 2026; `swap.svg` is Hugeicons' ArrowDataTransferVertical): `EditorController.swapActionEnds` runs `ActionGeometry.swappingEnds` (tested), which plays the action backwards in its own mode, filling an empty From from what it inherits, trading a curved Move's handles, and returning nil where there is no start and end (Show, Change Text, Play, presets, or a start the layer lacks), which hides the button. Easing is picked on the easing card (`Editor/EasingCard.swift`, raised by the Easing row beside the inspector, as in Jitter, the user's design on 28 September 2026): a grid of tiles (Custom first, then `EasingCard.order`), each drawing its curve (`EasingPath`), and under the pointer a tile not picked plays a small box moving with it (`EasingPreview`); a click picks and the card stays open; a second click on the picked tile (its settings icon shows under the pointer) turns the card to its graph and an Intensity slider (picking sets `Easing.standardIntensity`); Custom opens its graph at once, with the handles (`CurveEditor`) and its four numbers (`BezierFields`). The chosen tile uses `Theme.chosen`, not blue. An action's inspector has only its name in its header (the Back to Presets link and the header's New Action + went on 29 September 2026, the user's choice), and at its foot New Action and Select Preset as two small `PanelButton`s, one over the other (`AddAnother`; the user asked for a way to add another at the end, 28 September 2026, and for them small, since they are not the inspector's main thing), with a Swap Preset button in its own section above them while a preset bar is selected (29 September 2026): it opens the `PresetPicker` (`swapping`) on `swappablePresets`, and a pick runs `EditorController.swapPresets`, which turns each selected bar into it where it stands through `Presets.swapped` (tested: start, row and switch kept, the new settings, and its name, easing and length where they were still the old preset's own), one undo step; `AnimationInspector` puts the `PresetPicker` over whatever shows.
- **Turning along a path**: `MoveAction.turnsAlongPath` (left out of files when off; only a curved Move turns, `turns`), the "Rotate along path" switch under "Curved path". Rule 9 in `walk`: `ActionTracks.pathTurn` adds to the layer's rotation how far its path's direction has turned since the Move began (from its own angle, so nothing jumps; `turning` follows the curve in 24 steps, so bends past half a turn count), keeping the turn after the Move ends and stopping where a later Move takes over. Only layers in `turningLayers` pay for it.
- **Playback**: the canvas owns a `CADisplayLink` only while playing and calls `EditorController.advance(by:)`. The canvas and timeline representables read `editor.time` inside `updateNSView`, so a new playhead time updates them without re-running the whole window's SwiftUI. Bare keys (M, Space, comma and full stop with Shift or Option, Home, End) live in `EditorKeys`, handled by the canvas and the timeline, never as menu keys, so a letter typed in a field stays a letter.
- **Modes never share a control.** Animate is the main mode (2 October 2026, the user's choice): a project opens in it (`EditorModel.mode`) and it is the left of the switch, Design the right (`Mode`'s order). `EditorController.setMode` switches (and M does, through `EditorKeys`, never a menu key). Design: the canvas shows `evaluateDesign`, the inspector is `designContent` (layer, several layers, composition), handles and tools work, playback keys are off. Animate: the canvas shows `evaluate(at: time)`; since 5 October 2026 (the user's request) the drawing tools (shapes, frame, pen for new paths, text) work there too, from their keys only (no tool bar in Animate; the tool goes back to the arrow on entering Animate, and Escape puts any tool away in both modes), drawing into frames as they are at the playhead (`CanvasNSView.placed`, `Arrangement.placed(_:into:parentWorld:)`), and a selected layer's own handles resize and turn it there unless a Resize, Scale or Rotate action shows its own: worked out on the layer as it shows (`AnimateEditing.shown`), carried back into its design (`designed`), with its animation following (`EditorController.animatedUpdate`: Move actions as `layerUpdate` carries them, Rotate, Resize and Scale actions' To ends and starting values turned or grown by as much, `AnimateEditing.following`, tested). Its handles come before a selected Move's body drag. Double-click or Return retypes text and Option-drag copies (actions and all) in Animate too; editing points, cropping and the anchor stay Design's. Moves change the design position, and the inspector is `AnimationInspector` (Add Action, which uses `ActionFactory` for defaults at the playhead, and the selection's actions). The one design control Animate shares, as the user asked on 29 September 2026, is a sound's level: `LayerSoundSection` (the volume slider; its mute button went on 29 September 2026, as the user asked, and moving the slider unmutes a sound muted before) over the tiles when one sound or video with sound is selected, and under the fields when only its Play bars are (`ActionInspector(sound:)`). `EditorController.scene` follows the mode.
- **Editing actions**: `ActionInspector` shows the selected actions' fields (`TimingFields`: Start and Duration side by side, their names above the fields as `StackedRow`'s are, since names inside cut the numbers short, 30 September 2026; `EasingFields` with the custom `CurveEditor`, `ValueFields`); edits go through `EditorController.editActions` (one undo step) or `updateActions` (inside begin/end). `OptionalField` is a number that may be empty, showing the inherited value as a placeholder. `ActionGeometry` (MotionEditing, tested) turns points, sizes and angles into what an action stores, and switches To/By without changing the motion. `EditorController.canvasAction` is the one action the canvas edits: a Move shows its path (`CanvasOverlay.Motion`) with draggable ends and curve handles; dragging the layer sets the end from where it rests; the start dot over the layer needs Command. Resize, Scale and Rotate draw their outline and handles at the action's end state (`actionEndBox`) and seek there on grab. `layerUpdate` makes any design position change carry the layer's Move actions' absolute positions along.
- **Trim, dashes and dots** (2 October 2026, the user's request and choices): a trim is `TrimRange` (`direction`: `TrimDirection` start to end, end to start, both ends to middle, middle to both ends; `amount` 0 to 1), on `Stroke.trim` (left out of files when whole) and in `TrimAction` (both values share one direction, set from the action's Draws menu; interpolation takes the end's). Older files' start and end along the path are read as the nearest (`TrimRange.reading`; a `TrimAction` takes its direction from whichever value tells it, so old draw-ons and wipes play as before; a range in the middle becomes Middle to Both Ends). `TrimRange.pieces` are the stretches shown, and `PathTrim` (MotionEvaluation, tested) cuts the path into them by length (lines exactly, curves by a 16-step table), each run its own way or, `growing`, from where it starts to its tip; `ResolvedStroke.trimmed` holds them (nil for the whole path the way it runs, which is drawn from the shape's path as before) with `dashStarts`, and the renderer strokes `drawnPath` (each stretch dashed by `copy(dashingWithPhase:)` from its own place in the pattern, laid from the path's start so dashes stay put as a trim grows, since Core Animation would restart the pattern at every stretch); Core Animation's `strokeStart`/`strokeEnd` are no longer used. `Stroke.dotted` (Style: Solid, Dashed, Dotted, `StrokeLine` in `LayerInspector`) draws round dashes of no length, `[0, gap + width]`, the gap being `dashPattern[1]`. A Dashes action (`DashAction`: speed in px a second, `DashDirection`, `followsTrim`; the `dash` channel, `LayerState.dash` a `DashTravel`, By-like, eased as the bar) moves the first stroke's pattern; following the trim, the stretches run `growing` and the dashes travel towards each tip. It is offered only on a shape whose first stroke is dashed or dotted, and `addAction` makes it Linear and as long as the rest of the scene. The `dash.svg` icon is Hugeicons' DashedLine01. `TrimAndDashTests` and two pixel tests in `WarpRenderingTests` cover it.
- **Style and Blur actions** (Colour, Stroke, Trim, Corner Radius, Shadow, Layer Blur, Background Blur) are channels like any other in `ActionTracks`, with `LayerState` fields that are optional where a layer may not have the thing (`fill`, `stroke`, `trim`, `cornerRadii`); `resolve` in `Evaluate.swift` writes the state back onto the first fill, first stroke, rectangle corners and first switched-on shadow and blurs. Shadow and blurs start from `DropShadow.noShadow` / `BackgroundBlur.noBlur` / 0 when the design has none (never name these `none`: `.none` on an optional silently means nil). Paint blending (`Interpolation.swift`) pairs gradient stops when counts match and resamples at every stop position when they do not; a shadow with no opacity takes the other side's colour. `ActionFactory.kinds(for:)` decides what the menu offers (for several layers, what they all share, leaving out any sound selected with layers that draw, as the user asked, since a sound shares nothing with them; `addAction` and `addPreset` then skip layers that cannot take it); the menu groups by `ActionKind.category`. Inspector fields are `StyleValueFields`, with `PaintControls`, `ShadowControls` and `PaintSwatch` in `Components/PaintControls.swift`.
- **Motion blur** (29 September 2026, the user's choices: every kind of movement, a switch with an Amount, on the canvas and in export, each letter its own): `Action.motionBlur` is the shutter as a share of a frame (0.5 is 180°, `Action.standardMotionBlur`; nil is off and left out of files), set by `MotionBlurFields` under Easing, shown where `Action.canMotionBlur` (Move, Scale, Rotate, Skew, Tilt, and presets reaching those channels, `ActionTracks.moving`). `ActionTracks.motionBlur(of:at:frame:)` gives the moving channels and shutter of the blurred actions whose run meets the shutter (open half either side of the moment); `evaluate(_:at:motionBlur:)` (`MotionBlurQuality`: `.preview`, the default, 8 copies; `.export`, which `Exporter` passes, 16 exact moments) puts on each such layer a `MotionSpread` (boxed, `.none` otherwise): for the canvas the first copy's projection and the `step` to the next, from the layer at the shutter's start and one sample on with only the blurred channels moved, so other actions stay sharp; for export, once that is judged steady, `.exact`, every moment worked out; a text preset's letters get one each from `TextAnimation`. Exports never use the copies: `OffscreenRenderer.frame(of:into:bytesPerRow:)` draws `ResolvedScene.shutterScenes` (the scene at each moment, each blurred layer `.at` it) and averages them in floats with vDSP, rounding once (about 140 ms a blurred 1080p frame against 4 ms): the copies are each rounded to a whole step of 255 before Core Animation adds them, which in 8-bit exports turned an off-white card (247) white while it blurred, and averaging in pairs drifted as badly; the canvas (the window server) shows no such drift. The canvas draws it with `MotionCopies`: the layer's drawing wrapped (the first time it blurs, as the reveal holders are) in a `CAReplicatorLayer` drawing it `count` times, each `step` on in the layer's own space (a replicator turns about its origin), each at 1/count strength added with the private `plusL` filter, the replicator at 0.999 opacity so the copies form one picture before they are laid over the rest; the body is rasterised (`shouldRasterize`, at the layer's size on screen through `CoreAnimationRenderer.stretch`, held to `MotionCopies.maxPicture` pixels a side), so the copies are one picture repeated: the replicator otherwise drew every layer inside the body again for each copy, and a 500-layer group sliding in with blur (Revamp 07, 2 October 2026) took 130 to 410 ms a frame against 11 sharp; `motionBlurSmearsAMovingLayerAlongItsPath` checks the average and that it lays over, not adds to, what is behind. The container takes the first copy's transform, and the wrapping is taken off again the frame the blur ends (`updateMotion`). One step repeated only follows steady motion, so `MotionSpread` also takes the last copy's place and draws the frame sharp when repeating the step would miss it by more than a pixel and a fifth of the way (a Move jumping to its own From, a tilt turning from edge-on): before that, such a frame threw copies across the canvas for a frame (`motionThatIsNotSteadyIsDrawnSharp`). A background blur inside blurring copies sees nothing behind it, and masks do not blur. Split into Actions keeps the blur on the moving parts.
- **Z-Index** (4 November 2026, the user's request and choices): an action that draws a layer in front of or behind the layers beside it, only during playback, leaving the layers panel's order (the design) untouched. A plain number, not "in front of a named layer" (the user's choice over a relative form: the panel order is the hierarchy, and this is strictly an override): every layer is at 0, higher is drawn in front, lower behind, and the same number keeps the panel's order. `ActionSpec.zIndex(ZIndexAction)` (`index: Int`; file format 11), on its own channel (`Channel.zIndex`, `LayerState.zIndex`), always instant like Hide/Show (`ActionKind.isAlwaysInstant`, which `Action.isInstant`, the factory, the inspector's Duration field and the AI tools read): a later one takes over from its start, and before the first the layer is at 0. Only among siblings in the same group or frame (a group is flattened before it is drawn, so a layer cannot jump out of it); a group's own number moves it with everything inside. `evaluate` copies the number to `ResolvedLayer.zIndex` except in a frame in 3D, where depth decides, and Add Action does not offer it there (`ActionFactory.kinds(for:in:)`); a group inside a 3D frame is a flat card, so its children take it. Ordering is one key, `ResolvedLayer.drawingKey` (depth plus Z-Index, each 0 where the other counts): the renderer sets it as each container's `zPosition`, which Core Animation draws low to high with equal ones in list order, and `ResolvedScene.drawingOrder()` sorts by it, so canvas clicks and glass (which pictures what is drawn before it) follow it; background blur, through its backdrop layer, sees whatever is drawn behind at that moment. Masks keep the panel's order (the user expects a Z-Index on a whole masked group, not inside it). The inspector shows one whole-number field; the icon is Hugeicons' LayerBringToFront. Tested in `ZIndexTests`, `ZIndexRenderingTests` and `ActionFactoryTests`.
