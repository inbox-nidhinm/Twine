# Media and export

Decisions worth knowing about media and export, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` on 28 October 2026 so they load only when needed. Keep each entry up to date when its code changes.

- **Media**: `ProjectDocument` copies imports aside (`stage`, `stagedMedia`) and adds them to the package's `media` folder on save; `mediaURL(for:)` finds a file there or in the saved package. `MediaImport` probes and fingerprints files; `EditorController.importMedia` makes the layers (pictures as picture-filled rectangles) and Play actions (File > Import, and drops on `CanvasNSView`). A Play is an ordinary action (`.play(PlayAction(trimStart:))`) on the `media` channel: `ActionTracks.sourceTime` gives the frame (first before, last after), and `ActionEditing.trimmed` moves `trimStart` with the start edge and stops at the file's ends. `MediaLibrary`/`VideoFrames` (MotionRendering) decode frames in order with AVAssetReader, restarting only on a jump, for the canvas and export alike; decoded video is taken as sRGB whatever it is tagged, as exports write it. Pictures are converted to sRGB on load. Files no layer uses any more are let go (28 September 2026): `EditorController.releaseUnusedMedia` (`MediaLibrary.keep(only:)`, closing video decoders too, and `Waveforms.keep(only:)`) runs from `ProjectDocument`'s save-soon timer, a second after changes settle, undo and redo included; the files stay in the package, so an undo reads them again. The thumbnail borrows the editor's pictures (a save used to read every picture again) and keeps video decoders of its own (`Thumbnail.videos`, only the current project's), so it never sends the canvas's back to the start. `VideoFrames.restart` lets go of the old reader before making a new one, so one that fails to start is tried again on the next frame rather than leaving the layer grey. `Waveforms` awaits a file's tracks rather than blocking a thread on a semaphore. Waveforms mix every sound track, as export does. A clip's rotation (`preferredTransform`, counted y down) is applied between two flips in `VideoFrames.upright`, since Core Image counts y up: applied directly, portrait phone clips came out upside down (`VideoRotationTests` compares with AVFoundation's own). WebM, which macOS cannot open, is converted on import with the ffmpeg installed on the Mac (`Twine/Document/WebMConversion.swift`, the user's choice: ProRes 4444 when it has alpha, else HEVC or ProRes 422, every sound track; `EditorController.bringInWebM`, known again by the WebM's own fingerprint); no ffmpeg or a failed conversion says so in a notice. ffmpeg's output is read while it runs (`run`): read at the end, a stream of errors could fill the pipe and stall the import for ever. `audioClips(project)` (MotionEvaluation) is the sound, which `AudioComposition` lays out for AVFoundation: `AudioPreview` plays it following the playhead, limited exactly as an export is (4 October 2026: `LimitedMix`, MotionExport, reads the composition from a moment on through `AVAssetReaderAudioMixOutput` and `PeakLimiter`, stamping each piece with its place; an `AudioFeed` hands it to an `AVSampleBufferAudioRenderer` on its own queue, restarts flushing there so no old piece lands after, and the renderer plays on an `AVSampleBufferRenderSynchronizer`; an `AVPlayer` before had nothing to limit the sum). It is started with `setRate(_:time:atHostTime:)` 0.1 s ahead on the host clock the display link runs on (0.05 s left a jump's first piece 17 ms late; the kit's `audio` step logs how early it was); small drift is judged only after a settle, from the synchronizer's timebase, which costs nothing (the old player's `currentTime()` waited 0.7 s a frame on its lock with a dozen clips, 2 fps until 29 September 2026), jumps at once (judged from where and when it was set going), and nothing past where the sound runs out (the old player, stopped there, was sought every frame and on later loops never played again, which was the stutter). The test copy's own settings keep sound off (`playsSound`), so kit runs are silent; a muted renderer still plays its stream, `AudioMixdown` writes it (AAC in MP4, 16-bit PCM in MOV), reading the mix as 32-bit float and passing it through `PeakLimiter` (a look-ahead brickwall at −1 dBFS, tested: nothing over the ceiling, untouched below it, no gain jumps) so overlapping clips never clip, in the preview too. `AudioComposition` lays every sound track of a file, not only the first, and lists `unheard` media; export refuses to go on with any (`ExportError.soundUnreadable`, naming them) or when the mix fails, rather than writing a silent movie. The timeline draws waveforms from `Waveforms`, normalised to each file's loudest peak, and shaped by the layer's Volume actions. A Volume action (`VolumeAction`, 0 to 1, the `volume` channel, `LayerState.volume` starting from the design volume) becomes each clip's `envelope` of `VolumePoint`s (`volumeEnvelope`: a point just before and at each start, about thirty a second through each fade, so straight lines follow the easing), which `AudioComposition` turns into `setVolumeRamp`s, so preview and export hear the same fades. A sound or video and its Play bars are one thing (`MediaPlays`, MotionEditing, tested): `EditorController.deleteSelectedActions` goes through `MediaPlays.deleting`, which also deletes a sound or video whose last Play goes, and `ProjectDocument.read` runs `MediaPlays.withPlayBars`, giving one without a bar a Play from 0 as long as its file. Volume and the sound presets are only offered where `Project.hasSound(_:)` (a sound, or a video whose asset `hasAudio`): use `ActionFactory.kinds(for:in:)` and `Presets.available(for:in:)`, which know the media; the plain `kinds(for:)` takes `hasSound`. The Design Sound section hides for a video without sound. Volume stops at 100% everywhere (the Design slider and the action, as the user asked); older projects saved above it are heard at 100% (`min` in `LayerState(design:)` and `audioClips`).
- **GIF and WebM** (30 September 2026): `ExportFormat.gif` and `.webm` are movies (`isMovie`: frame rate and range apply), `carriesSound` false for GIF, `needsFFmpeg` for WebM (the menu shows it disabled with "Needs ffmpeg" when `WebMConversion.tool` finds none; `ExportSession` passes that ffmpeg to `Exporter.export(ffmpeg:)`). A GIF is at most `ExportFormat.gifMaxRate` (50 fps, applied in `ExportSettings.exported`, since frame times are hundredths and browsers slow anything under two), its delays rounded from exact times so they never drift. `GIFWriter` (MotionExport, `GIFExportTests`) is our own, frame by frame: the system's GIF destination keeps every frame to the end (4.2 GB for 8 s of 1080p). Each frame has its own 256-entry palette (`GIFPalette`: colours covering at least 0.2% of what it draws kept exactly, up to 64, since median cut alone gave an uncovered background a shade off and left a trail; the rest median cut over a 5-bit histogram), a 4 by 4 ordered dither only where a colour is far from its palette entry (gradients, not flat colour), index 255 see-through. On an opaque ground only pixels that changed are drawn, in their box, the rest see-through over the kept frame (disposal 1); on a transparent ground each frame is whole (disposal 2), pixels under half opaque see-through, colour unpremultiplied. `GIFLZW` follows gif.h (a clear first, the trie stamped with a generation so clearing is a counter). About 18 ms a 1080p frame in release; the debug test copy is far slower, so time GIFs in a release build. WebM pipes raw BGRA frames to ffmpeg (`libvpx-vp9`, CRF 30, `yuva420p` when transparent with `-auto-alt-ref 0`, BT.709 through `scale=out_color_matrix`), the sound mixed first by `AudioMixdown` into a WAV beside it and made Opus; SIGPIPE is ignored so a failed ffmpeg becomes an error rather than ending the app.
- **Export**: `Exporter.export` blocks, so `ExportSession` (one per window, on `EditorModel.exports`) runs it on a background queue from a copy of the project, with settings saved to `UserDefaults`. The inspector's `PrimaryButton` (at its foot, both modes) and ⌘E call `EditorController.openExport`, which raises `ExportPanel` (`EditorModel.showExport`) over the whole window: settings on the left; on the right an `ExportPreview` (its own `CoreAnimationRenderer`, sharing the canvas's `MediaLibrary`) driven by an `ExportPreviewPlayer` (its own display link, playhead snapped to the export's frames, its own `AudioPreview`), which rises out as the `SlideCounter` comes up (`Theme.exportEase`, cubic-bezier(0.5, 0, 0, 1)). The counter is Newsreader Extra Light (`ThemeFont.display(size, .extraLight)`; the display face is Newsreader since 26 October 2026, the user's choice, replacing PP Kyoto: one variable font, `Newsreader.ttf` under the SIL Open Font License, set by `ThemeFont` to each weight and to the optical size of the size it is drawn at) and counts as an odometer (the user's choice, 30 September 2026; the whole number and its percent sign slid together before): each digit place is a `RollingDigit` whose digit, keyed by `.id`, rises one type size and fades out as the next rises from one type size below and fades in, on `Theme.counterEase`, and the percent sign stays still; places are keyed from the right, so a new place (10, 100) rolls in with the same transition. The places roll together from one whole number to the next (`SlideCounter.shown`, a roll always finishing and the newest number following it): rolled one by one, the ones place still finishing its last roll showed 109 on the way to 100. The number is held to 0...100. Its digits sit in boxes of the widest digit's width (`Figures`), since the face has no tabular figures. `ExportKeys` makes the canvas and timeline swallow keys while it is up (Escape closes, Space plays). `closeExport` cancels an export in flight and forgets `ExportSession.completed` (the file Export Again and Show in Finder refer to). Quality is forced to High in `ExportSession.begin`, as the user asked. `ExportSettings.frameRate` (nil for the composition's) is applied by `Exporter.export` through `exported(_:)`, and a range's frames are counted at that rate. `ExportStatus`, the small card at the top, now only shows for exports made outside the panel (Export Current Frame). The panel's Export button calls `ExportSession.request`, which the window controller sets, rather than sending an action through the responder chain, which misses when the window is not key. Files are written in a replacement folder and moved into place when complete. However the frame loop stops, the sound's reader is cancelled and the writer too (one `catch` round the loop), and `AudioMixdown.wait(until:)` gives up once the export is cancelled or the writer has failed, since the writer may never ask for more. `SoundTimingTests` checks that a click lands on its own sample in a range and that a sound shorter than its bar (as a phone clip's often is) still exports. Formats without alpha export over the background colour; formats with alpha store straight (unpremultiplied) colour, unpremultiplied with vImage, since ProRes specifies it and editing apps and ffmpeg expect it. Colour is tagged Rec. 709. A PNG in Design mode is the design (`actions` emptied), otherwise the frame at the playhead. Check exports with `ffprobe` and `ffmpeg` (in `/opt/homebrew/bin`) rather than only AVFoundation, which agrees with itself. `ExportSettings.scale` is any multiple (the size menu offers 0.5, 1, 2 and Custom…, which shows a Scale field taking a percentage), held by `effectiveScale` to 4K (`maxLongSide` 3840, `maxShortSide` 2160, either way round); `pixelSize` is the only place sizes are worked out, rounding movies to even pixels, which cannot pass 4K as its sides are even. A range (`ExportSession.range`, per window, unsaved) becomes `frames` for `Exporter.export`: `ExportTiming` starts the movie at zero from `firstFrame`, and `AudioMixdown` reads from the range's start and shifts each sample back by it.
- **Lottie** (13 November 2026, the user's choices: exact motion, effects Lottie cannot draw left out and named, both JSON and dotLottie): `ExportFormat.lottie` (`.json`, pictures as base64 inside) and `.dotLottie` (`.lottie`, a zip of `manifest.json`, `animations/animation.json` and `images/`, the format's first version, which dotlottie-web and every player open; `ZipWriter` is our own, deflating with the Compression framework). Both are `isMovie` (range and frame rate apply) and `isLottie` (no size menu, no sound). `LottieWriter` (MotionExport/Lottie) evaluates every frame as a movie export does and records each layer (`Recording`, a value kept once while unchanged), then plans Lottie layers: every one sits in the composition's own space, its transform the layer's flat world transform split into Lottie's position, scale, rotation and skew (`LottieTransform`, rotation unwrapped between frames; a tilted or 3D layer gets the flat transform nearest its four corners, `LottieGeometry.flat`). Properties are thinned to the keyframes straight lines cannot stand in for (`Series`, a swinging-door sweep, tolerances of a few thousandths). A group gets no layer of its own unless it must draw as one picture (opacity under 100% at any frame, a blend mode, a clip, a mask or a Mask preset); then it is a precomp the composition's size, so nothing is cut off, with its clip and reveal as Lottie masks. Twine masks are alpha mattes placed straight above the layer (a masked leaf is wrapped in a precomp first); picture fills are image layers matted by the outline; inside and outside strokes are drawn twice as wide and matted by the outline (inverted for outside), as Lottie strokes only straddle the path. Text is letter outlines (`LottieText`, placed as `TextImage` places glyphs), one shape layer per distinct part: the whole text, the one leaving in a swap (its transition rebuilt as the renderer does it), or each letter, word or line a preset moves. Animated paths keep one point count and one open or closed state for the whole animation (`LottieGeometry.uniform`): lottie-web keeps the first keyframe's count and drew nothing when a trim changed it. Left out and named in `ExportOutcome.leftOut` (shown under Show in Finder in the panel): shadows, blur, background blur, glass, motion blur, page curls, 3D/tilt/bend, video, angular gradients (drawn as their average colour), oval radial gradients (drawn round), tiled pictures (drawn once), picture strokes, Linear Burn (Multiply), and drawing order that changes over time (siblings keep the first frame's order). Checked against lottie-web 5.12.2 and dotlottie-web 0.38.2 with `LottieComparison` (`TWINE_LOTTIE_OUT=folder swift test --filter LottieComparison` writes a scene of one of everything with Twine's own frames beside it): shapes land exactly, text within half a pixel (Twine's text picture snaps to pixels).
