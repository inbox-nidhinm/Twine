# Layers and how they look

Decisions worth knowing about layers and how they look, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` on 28 October 2026 so they load only when needed. Keep each entry up to date when its code changes.

- **Frames** are groups with a look: `GroupContent.frame` (`FrameStyle`: fills, strokes, corner radii; nil for a plain group), so everything groups do (nesting, grouping, ungrouping, carrying actions) holds. `Layer.isFrame`; `Layer.look` is the fills, strokes and outline of a shape, or of a frame as a rectangle, and is what the inspector's Fill and Stroke sections, `LayerState(design:)`, pictures and cropping read and write. `evaluate` resolves a frame's look into `ResolvedLayer.frame`; `ResolvedScene.bounds(of:)` is a frame's own box. The renderer's `Node` keeps a group's children in `children`, a holder inside the container, clipped there (to the box for a group, to the frame's outline by `clipMask`), so a clipping group keeps its shadow; a frame's container holds its fills, then the holder, then its strokes (`updateShape(around:)`). Frames resize (`CanvasGeometry.resized`), are hit on their own fill and stroke, and take Resize, Colour, Stroke, Corner Radius and the blurs (`ActionFactory`); hit testing honours any clipping parent (`shows`). `LayerFactory.frame` is Figma's default (white, clipping). The Frame tool (`Tool.frame`, F, `Tool.draws`) draws one, and at mouse up takes in the siblings wholly inside it (`Arrangement.enclosed`, `adopt`); anything drawn with a press inside a frame goes into the innermost one (`CanvasNSView.frame(under:)`, `Arrangement.placed`), text too. `Arrangement.group(asFrame:)` is Frame Selection (⌥⌘G). Grouping moves layers in with `.moveLayers` rather than deleting and reinserting them. `Arrangement.move` (the layers panel's drops) keeps layers in place on screen across parents, carrying their actions.
- **Blend modes and shadows**: `Layer.blendMode` (Figma's list, `BlendMode.groups` for the menu; left out of the file at Normal) is the container's `compositingFilter` from `Blending` (Core Animation's private filters by name; all 17 match the W3C formulas Figma uses to within 3% in `BlendRenderingTests`, except Soft Light, Pegtop's formula, up to 5% darker in dark colours; Core Image's was further off). A group at Normal passes its children's blending through, as Figma's Pass Through. The composition's background is the renderer's `contentLayer`'s own colour, under the layers: a blend only sees what is drawn in its own superlayer, and while the background was a layer beside them (until 29 September 2026) every mode drew as Normal over it (`blendModesBlendWithTheBackground`). A mask's own node ignores blend (`ignoresBlend`). `EffectKind.innerShadow` (shapes and frames). `ResolvedLayer.shadows` lists every drop shadow on, the first carrying the Shadow action (`state.shadow`); `innerShadows` likewise, the first carrying the Inner Shadow action (4 October 2026: `ActionSpec.innerShadow`, a `ShadowAction`, the `innerShadow` channel, `LayerState.innerShadow` boxed as `InnerShadow` since most layers have none; from nothing when the design has none, shapes and frames only). A shape or frame that draws casts all its shadows from its outline (fills and strokes as regions, `Node.outline`, cached by look), each a `ShadowLayer` drawing only its shadow with the outline knocked out, at the fills' strength (`Node.strength`: a 25% card casts a 25% shadow), as Figma does; text, video, plain groups and frames that draw nothing keep one shadow on the container, from what they draw. An `InnerShadowLayer` is a sheet with the outline (shrunk by spread) cut out, casting inward, masked to the outline, over the fills. `Node.arrange` stacks a shape's or frame's sublayers (shadows, backdrop, fills, inner shadows, a frame's children, strokes), rebuilding only when their counts change.
- **Pictures are fills**, as in Figma: there is no picture layer. `Paint.image(ImagePaint)` (media id, `ImageScaleMode` fill, fit, crop or tile, `crop` as an `ImageCrop` in fractions of the box, `quarterTurns`, `tileScale`, `opacity`) is `indirect`, as is `ResolvedPaint.image`, because a larger paint made every layer state dearer to copy (evaluate went from 1.18 to 1.35 ms until it was boxed). `ImagePlacement` (MotionEvaluation, tested) is the only place a picture's box is worked out, from `Project.pictureSizes` (the assets' pixel sizes): every box is the picture after its turns. `evaluate` hands the renderer `ResolvedImage` (frame, turns, tiles, opacity); `PaintLayers` draws it in a holder masked by the shape, tiles with two `CAReplicatorLayer`s (at most 256 a side), and shapes with a picture skip `Node`'s unchanged check, as video does. Strokes never take a picture; a Colour action is not offered when the first fill is one, and picture paints hold rather than blend. `Layer.picture` is the first picture fill, which the inspector (`PictureFields`), Replace and cropping use; `Layer.mediaIDs` lists every file a layer uses. `LayerFactory.media` makes a picture a rectangle filled with it.
- **Replace and cropping**: `PictureEditing` (MotionEditing, tested) holds the maths: `replacingPicture` (a crop keeps its centre and covers what showed, in the new picture's shape; a layer still named after its old file takes the new name), `replacingVideo` (pulls a play's `trimStart` back so it ends by the new clip's end), `settingMode` (into Crop, the crop that shows the picture where it was), `turned`, `movedCrop`, `scaledCrop` (keeps shape, opposite corner fixed), `croppingResize` (box handles crop, the picture stays put). `EditorController.replaceMedia(of:)` opens the panel; `replaceMedia(of:with:)` and `fillWithPicture(_:from:)` take a URL (the test kit calls these) and `bringIn` reuses a file already in the project by its hash. Cropping is `Canvas/CanvasCropping.swift`: a `CropSession`, entered by `setPictureMode(.crop)` (the inspector) or a double-click on a picture, left by Escape, Return, a click away from the picture and the box, or `validateCropSession` (selection, mode, tool). Presses are `CanvasDrag.CropEdit` (move, a picture corner, a box handle); picture corners win where they meet the box's. The overlay draws the whole picture at `CanvasOverlay.cropPictureOpacity` in `cropHolder` (the overlay sizes its own sublayers), a dashed outline and corner squares. Videos have `VideoContent.fit` (Fill or Fit, `contentsGravity`), decoded as Fill from older files.
- **Fills, strokes and effects are lists** so a Figma import keeps everything. Fills are stored bottom first, and the fill that counts is the top one (`ShapeContent.topFillIndex`, the user's choice on 28 September 2026): the Colour action drives it (`LayerState.fill`, written back in `resolve`), and `pictureIndex` (Replace, cropping, the layers panel's picture icon) is the topmost picture. The inspector lists every fill, every stroke and every effect (4 October 2026, the user's choice: fills and strokes laid out as Effects are), top first for fills and strokes (both draw in list order, the last on top): the header's + adds one on top (a second fill is black at 20%; a new stroke copies the top one's width and settings in the stroke colour), and each has an `InspectorItemTitle` name row with its own −. A layer keeps one picture fill, so Image is offered only on that fill or while there is none (`fillWithPicture(_:at:)`, by fill id). `StrokeSettings` edits its stroke by id. Stroke, Trim and Dashes actions still drive the first stroke (the bottom one), and the Shadow and Inner Shadow actions the first of each.
- **Gradients** (4 October 2026, the user's request: stops at will and a handle on the canvas, as in Figma): `Gradient` already held any stops and a start and end as fractions of the box (a radial one's start its centre, its end on its edge). `Paint.angularGradient` (file format 4, the same day) goes a whole turn clockwise round its start from the direction of its end, drawn with Core Animation's `.conic` gradient, whose angle is in the layer's pixels (`GradientRenderingTests`); `Paint.gradient`, `gradientKind` (`GradientKind`) and `withGradient` are the model's, so code reads a gradient without listing the kinds. `GradientEditing` (MotionEditing, tested) is the maths: stops keep their index when moved past each other (`order` is for showing them by place), a new stop takes the colour already there (`color(of:at:)`), the two ends' stops cannot be removed (`fewestStops`), `snapped` keeps an end to 15° steps. The inspector's `GradientStopsEditor` (`Components/GradientControls.swift`, in `PaintEditor` for fills and strokes and in `PaintControls` for Colour and Stroke actions): a bar of the gradient with a marker per stop (a click adds a stop, a marker drags; its presses are an AppKit view, `GradientBarCatcher`, so posted clicks land), the picked stop's place beside it (a place on every row left the hex no room), then a row per stop by place with colour, hex and opacity, and a remove button while there are more than two; editing a stop picks it. `Canvas/CanvasGradient.swift` shows the line whenever the one layer selected in Design (not tilted) has a gradient, its top fill's or else its stroke's (the user's choice: a switch for it came first and went the same day), over the layer's own handles, which it wins where they meet: ends drag (Shift for 15° steps), the line moves the whole, a stop's dot slides it, a click on the line adds a stop, Delete removes the picked stop (beeping at two rather than deleting the layer; with none picked, Delete deletes the layer, and any press off the line lets the stop go), Command passes through to the anchor, and Escape hides it (`EditorModel.hidesGradient`) until the selection or mode changes. In Animate (4 October 2026) the same line edits the `canvasAction`'s gradient when it is a Colour or Stroke action: its end, or its starting value when only that is a gradient or it is picked (`PaintSlot.action(id, initial:)`, `Action.paint(initial:)` and `with(_:initial:)` in `GradientControls.swift`), placed on the layer as evaluated at the action's end (or start), where a press takes the playhead, as a Resize's handles are; `GradientPlace.command` writes either kind. `EditorModel.gradient` (`GradientEdit`: layer, `PaintSlot`, picked stop) is the stop picked on the bar or the canvas. Each drag is one undo step. Radial and angular gradients show their circle. A radial one may be an oval (4 October 2026, as Figma's third handle): `Gradient.across` (nil is 1, a circle, and left out of files; `aspect` reads and writes it, held to `Gradient.aspects`) is its reach across its line as a share of its reach along it, both in the layer's pixels; `GradientEditing.acrossPoint`/`reachingAcross` are the third handle's maths, and the canvas draws the oval turned with its line. `ResolvedPaint.radial` carries the centre, edge and aspect; Core Animation draws only level ovals, so a turned one is drawn by `PaintLayers.placeTurned` (the gradient layer laid out where the oval is a circle, turned and squashed into place, the masking shape carried back through the inverse). `Gradient.radial(center:edge:edgeAcross:in:)` makes one from two axes, as Figma pastes and SVGs give them (an SVG's box-unit circle in a box that is not square comes in as an oval). The `fill-angular` icon is Hugeicons' PieChart09.
- **Copy and Paste Style** (4 October 2026, Figma's Copy and Paste Properties, ⌥⌘C and ⌥⌘V, which moved Copy and Paste Easing to ⇧⌘C and ⇧⌘V at the user's choice): `LayerStyle` (MotionEditing, `StyleCopying.swift`, tested) is what is copied: opacity, blend mode and effects for every layer but a sound, fills and strokes from a shape or frame, a text's type (font, weight, size, alignment, spacing), colour and stroke, never its words; between text and shapes the colour and stroke carry across (a shape's top fill to a text's colour, one colour for a text's stroke). `applied(to:media:)` gives everything new ids and leaves out picture fills whose file the project lacks. `EditorController.copyStyle(of:)` writes it on its own pasteboard type; `pasteStyle` works in Design only (a style is the design), on every selected unlocked layer, through `FileSafety.numbersHeld` and `held`, as one undo step. Also on a layer's right-click menu in Design.
- **Arc and Star** live in the geometry: `.ellipse(arc: EllipseArc)` and `.star(points:innerRadius:cornerRadius:)`, both decoding older files with defaults.
- **Corner radius on any shape** (7 October 2026, the user's request, as Figma's): `.polygon(sides:cornerRadius:)` and `PathPoint.cornerRadius` (each point its own, left out of files when 0, so older files read as sharp). `ShapeGeometry.points(size:)` is what points are edited by (`PathEditing.path(of:)`): rounded corners as one sharp point carrying its radius (a rectangle's four, a polygon's or star's every point); `bezierPath(size:)` is as drawn, through `BezierPath.roundingCorners()` (`MotionEvaluation/PathCorners.swift`, tested in `PathCornerTests`: a corner's curve starts `radius / tan(angle / 2)` along each side, held to half of each side, curved sides cut by length, smooth points and open ends untouched; a rectangle still draws through `ShapePaths.rectangle`, which it matches exactly). `resolve` rounds pen paths after fitting them. `CornerEditing` (MotionEditing, tested) sets a radius on picked points or all: a rectangle stays one, a polygon or star stays one while every point changes together and otherwise becomes a path, and a path's Path actions take the new radius where they had the old. The canvas publishes the picked points as `EditorModel.pickedPoints` (a turn later, from `pathSession`'s `didSet`), which the inspector's Radius field (`cornerRadiusField`, an `OptionalField` dimming the largest when they differ) edits through `EditorController.setCornerRadius`. The point-editing outline is drawn rounded. The Corner Radius action takes polygons (one radius, `topLeft`); a Path action blends each point's radius.
- **Masks** (Figma's since 4 October 2026, the user's request): `Layer.isMask` (left out of files when off) makes a layer mask the layers in front of it in the same group or frame, up to the next mask; it does not draw itself. Use as Mask (`Masking.useAsMask`, MotionEditing, tested; the layers' right-click menu, Edit > Use as Mask, ⌃⌘M) makes one layer a mask where it is, or puts several (sharing a parent, as Group needs) into a "Mask group", the one at the back the mask, and selects the group; the inspector's Mask section shows only on a mask, its one button Remove (`Masking.removeMask`, the group stays). A group or a sound cannot be a mask (`Layer.canMask`: a mask is drawn alone, as one layer), and Use as Mask says so in a notice. There is no Invert (the user's choice, matching Figma). Before, each masked layer named its mask (`MaskReference`, kept only as `Layer.legacyMask`, read from older files and never written); `MaskConversion.converted` (from `ProjectDocument.read`) makes each old mask a Mask group with the layers it masked, a copy of the mask with its actions for each further place, dropping inversion, and file format 5 marks the change. `evaluate` notes a mask as it walks each list, gives every later sibling a `ResolvedMask` naming it, leaves mask layers out of `ResolvedScene.layers` and puts them in `ResolvedScene.masks` (found by `layer(_:)` too, so a selected mask gets its box), ignoring solo; `ResolvedMask.transform` carries the mask's space into the masked layer's (`WorldTransforms`). A hidden mask still ends the one before it but masks nothing. The renderer draws each mask with a `MaskNode` of its own set as the masked container's `mask`. Canvas hit testing honours masks, and picks a mask only while it is selected. Figma's masks come in as they are; an SVG's `clip-path` or mask goes into a Mask group with its element, so it masks only that.
- **Blur** is in Figma's units everywhere in the model: twice the Gaussian's standard deviation (CSS's shadow blur). The renderer halves it for Core Animation. `MotionRendering/Blur.swift` uses Core Animation's private `CAFilter` `gaussianBlur` and `CABackdropLayer`, looked up by name, because `CIGaussianBlur` draws about 9 px off (at radius 12) on screen and off, and `backgroundFilters` only see siblings, not what is behind a nested layer. If either class is missing, layer blur falls back to Core Image and background blur is skipped. Every private filter is made through `PrivateFilter.make`, which checks the class answers `filterWithType:` before calling it; the `destOut` filter is made once. Setting the filter's `inputNormalizeEdges` makes it draw nothing in `CARenderer`. The backdrop sits under a shape's paint layers, masked to its path, with opacity as the blend.
- **Glass** (19 October 2026, the user's request, as Figma's; file format 10; the long version is `docs/glass.md`): `EffectKind.glass(Glass)` (MotionModel: `lightAngle` in degrees on Figma's dial, 0 the top and -45 the top left; `lightIntensity`, `refraction`, `dispersion`, `splay` 0 to 1; `depth` and `frost` in pixels; `Glass()` is Figma's new glass, `noGlass` none, `held` the panel's ranges), shapes and frames only, the first one switched on counting; the Glass action (`GlassAction`, the `glass` channel, category Blur, `LayerState.glass` boxed as `GlassState`, `Glass.interpolate` turning the light the shorter way) and `ResolvedLayer.glass` (held). Drawn with the pieces macOS 26 builds its own Liquid Glass from (`MotionRendering/Glass.swift`, whose header holds the spike's findings): a `CABackdropLayer` through Core Animation's `displacementMap` (Refraction), under a second backdrop through `chromaticAberrationMap` (Dispersion) and `gaussianBlur` (Frost), each reading its own `CASDFLayer` sublayer named `glassField` (a distance field of the outline through a `CASDFGlassDisplacementEffect`: `height` is Depth, `curvature` 1 + 3 × Splay); two backdrops because a map filter after another filter on one backdrop reads its map out of place (a cyan band along the top and bottom, the bend gone). Over the fills two `CASDFLayer`s with `CASDFGlassHighlightEffect` light the rim from the angle and, at 60%, the opposite side. The field's element is `bounds` mode for a box with every corner alike (`ResolvedShape.roundedBox`, set for uniform rectangles and circles; exact, free) and otherwise a picture (`GlassField.picture`: red the signed distance between `contentsZeroValueDistance` and `contentsOneValueDistance`, green and blue the way out to the edge, 16-bit and tagged sRGB, or the values are converted and the edge moves in; worked out by exact distance to the outline's pieces near the edge, 3 to 5 ms even full screen, rows turned over when `flipsContents`). Backdrops, fields and elements reach `GlassKit.margin` past the box: a field cut off at the edge bent one side only. A field no filter reads draws itself as a blue card and one hidden bends nothing, so each is shown exactly while read. The light's angle needs no turning over off screen. On screen a backdrop shows whatever is on the screen where it lies, layers of other windows, the Dock and the menu bar included, and no group, filter, opacity or rasterising around the canvas stops it (all four tried on 20 October 2026): Depth 639 on a 248 point circle, with the backdrop reaching twice the depth past the box, turned the middle inside out and showed the screen around Twine, live (the user's report). So Depth goes to `Glass.maxDepth` (100, the user's choice; `held` and the inspector, which takes a larger typed number as 100), `GlassKit.fitted` holds it to the outline's own thickness (half the box's shorter side for a rounded box, else `GlassField.thickness`, measured on a grid and cached: a star's thin points scrambled its middle at Depth 100, `thinShapesNeverTurnInsideOut`), and `GlassKit.margin` reaches the depth, the frost's reach (1.5 times the frost, at most `frostReach`, 600) and 8 more past the box. Zoomed in, glass near the window's edge still bent in the menu bar and the Dock, so what a backdrop looks at is cut to `CoreAnimationRenderer.visibleArea` (`GlassLayers.clip(to:)`, `glassLooksOnlyAtWhatTheCanvasShows`): the canvas sets it from its whole view (between the panels only left a hard line where the bend stopped round the tool bar) and the composition, on every zoom, pan and resize (`layoutWorld`), the export preview to the composition; `clipGlass` carries it into each glass layer's own points through `worldProjection`, and a pan moves only the backdrops, never rebuilding their filters. Nil off screen. All that was still not enough on screen (the top of the window under the title bar, and the bottom, still showed the screen), so since 21 October 2026 (v0.2.41, the user's choice; exports too since v0.2.43) glass draws what is behind it itself (`CoreAnimationRenderer.drawsGlassBehind`, on in `CanvasNSView`, `ExportPreviewView` and `OffscreenRenderer`; `drawGlassBehind`, `GlassBehind`): every layer drawn before the glass in the screen's order (`drawingOrder`, so in a 3D frame by depth), its holding groups at full strength (`atFullStrength`, or a faded group's glass came out white or faded twice), and `glassOnly` (the layer as only its glass, light off) are drawn through backdrops by a renderer of their own into `IOSurface` pictures (one the screen is not using, `isInUse`), upright over the composition at the canvas's pixels a point and no more of it than the canvas shows and the glass reaches (`visibleArea`, snapped to quarters of the view; a change redraws the glass), on a queue of its own with `async` and a semaphore (never `sync`, which runs on the calling thread, where a flush inside the canvas's layout transaction committed nothing and the picture came out at its old size), and shown by one plain holder clipped a pixel past the outline (`GlassLayers.show`), so the canvas is the export and backdrops never look at the screen; a zoom draws the scene again a turn later (`redrawSoon`: zooming changes no project, so nothing redrew and the glass kept its old picture until a click); `Glass.interpolate` gives a setting that shows nothing at one end the other end's (no light swinging as glass fades); open paths are not offered Glass; `docs/glass.md` has the traps. The field picture is worked out at the canvas's pixels a point (`pixelScale`, its zoom up to 8 included, rounded up to a power of two so a pinch draws it again only as the zoom doubles; never past `GlassField.largestSide`, 2048, 20 to 50 ms in release): at one texel a point, a small star zoomed in bent in blotches; a rounded box needs none, so the zoom never rebuilds its glass. Without the private classes the glass is its frost alone. `GlassRenderingTests` checks pixels (`TWINE_GLASS_OUT` writes them); the canvas was checked by eye in the test copy, whose `zoom F` and `pan DX DY` steps zoom and pan it (a window shot never shows what a backdrop pulls in from outside the window: capture the screen with the test copy in front, `screencapture -x`, which comes out black while the Mac is locked). The app says `Glass` through a typealias to MotionModel's (`PaintControls.swift`), since SwiftUI has a `Glass` of its own; `GlassControls` is the panel for both the effect and the action. The Figma plugin copies `GLASS` effects (its frost is `radius`; Figma's plugin API has no Splay yet), read by `FigmaImport.effect`; the angle is taken as Figma's dial gives it, not yet checked against Figma itself.
