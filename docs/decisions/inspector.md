# The inspector

Decisions worth knowing about the inspector, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` on 28 October 2026 so they load only when needed. Keep each entry up to date when its code changes.

- **The layer inspector** (Design, 28 September 2026, as the user laid it out): Position (`AlignRow`: the horizontal and vertical alignments in two trays, as Figma's, and a menu for distributing; then X and Y, rotation beside the `AnchorPicker`), Layout (W, a link, H; SX, a link, SY, always both; corner radius, sides, points or arc; Clip content as a `CheckRow`), Appearance (`BlendOpacityRow`: the blend menu beside the opacity field, shared with several layers), then Fill, Stroke, Text, Effects, Mask. The width and height link is `Layer.keepsProportions` (off for new layers, left out of files when off; Figma's constrain proportions): typing either changes the other, and canvas resizing keeps proportions as Shift does (never for text). The scale link is `Transform.isScaleLinked`. Figma's constraints were offered and declined on 28 September 2026 (layout only). Each stroke is its name row, its colour, then the alignment menu, the width and a settings button raising `StrokeSettings` (trim, ends, corners, dashes) on a `PopupCard`, which reads the layer afresh so it follows edits. Effects each show their action's icon, in their rows and in the section's add menu. Nothing inside the inspector is blue: chosen segments and switches that are on use `Theme.chosen`.
- **Several layers selected** (Design): Position holds only the alignments, and Appearance the blend and opacity. When every selected layer is a text, the Text and Fill sections follow (10 November 2026, after the user could not change several texts at once): the same `TextSection` and `TextFillSection` as for one text, given a list, each change going to all of them as one undo step (`EditorController.editTexts`). A value they do not share reads Mixed (`UnitField`'s and `OptionalField`'s `mixed`, the menus' and the font button's text); typing, nudging or dragging a Mixed number starts from the first text's. Fill shows the first text's colour: a new colour keeps each text's own opacity, and a new opacity keeps each one's colour. Stroke is not offered for several texts yet, nor Text for a mix of texts and other layers.
- **The inspector's grid** (the user's Figma, Page 4 of `RRnath7INm7WH7CGLOfLdH`, 28 September 2026): every section body is `.gridBody()` (the panel inset on the left; on the right `Theme.buttonInset` plus `Theme.rowGutter`, a button column one control wide and `Theme.columnGap`), so every row stops at the same edge and the column lines up with the headers' add and remove buttons, which `HeaderRow` also places at `Theme.buttonInset`. A button that acts on one row sits in the column level with it through `.rowButton { }` (which reaches out into the gutter and holds the column's width even when its button is hidden): the distribute menu, the width/height and scale links, the corners button, the picture's quarter turn, show/hide (a fill's or stroke's on its first row, an effect's on its first row of values), the stroke's settings, and in Animate the links and a starting value's clear button (`StackedRow`'s accessory). Section headers hold only add; a fill's, stroke's or effect's remove is on its name row. A section ends with `.sectionPadding(empty:)`: 12 pt under its rows, or, when it shows only its header (no stroke, no effects, no mask), half above and half below so the name is centred between the dividers; the layer inspector ends with a divider. An ellipse's Start, Sweep (as a share of a full turn) and Ratio are one `JoinedFields` box with the arc icon, as Figma's.
- **Dragging numbers** (28 September 2026): every `UnitField` and `OptionalField` has a `ScrubSurface` laid over it while not being typed in (an AppKit view, so posted clicks land and the pointer stays `Cursors.resizeHorizontal`): a drag past 3 points changes the number by `step` a point (hundredths for seconds, 1 otherwise; Shift ten times, Option a tenth), resting at the last value `accepts` allows; a click gives the field the keyboard with its number selected. Each step is the field's ordinary `commit`; the pointer never meets the screen's edge (5 October 2026, as in Figma: `ScrubPointer` hides the real pointer and holds it with `CGAssociateMouseAndMouseCursorPosition`, the number follows the mouse's deltas, a stand-in window of the same arrows wraps to the far side past an edge, and letting go warps the real pointer there; a local mouse-up monitor and `viewDidMoveToWindow` always let go; only real drags with movement of their own lock, never posted ones, and never in the test copy, which holds the user's real mouse otherwise, so the kit cannot check it); the drag is one undo step because `EditorView` sets `\.scrubbing` to `EditorController.scrubbing`, which opens a folding continuous change (`ProjectStore.beginContinuousChange(_:folding:)`, in which `perform` joins the change instead of recording). The test kit's `clear` presses Delete wherever the keyboard is, so a click that misses a field makes it delete the selection.
- **Arrow keys in number fields** (the user's request, 30 September 2026; they asked for eight with Shift, where Figma's default is ten): a focused `UnitField` or `OptionalField` takes Up and Down through `.onKeyPress` (which reaches SwiftUI's field while its field editor has the keyboard; the kit's `key up`/`key down` showed it), stepping from the typed number by `ArrowNudge.amount`: the field's own `step` when given, else a tenth of a second for times and 1 for everything else, times `ArrowNudge.shiftTimes` (8) with Shift, rounded to the field's places. Each press is its own commit, so its own undo step.
- **Fields**: typed text is lifted by `Theme.fieldTextLift` screen pixels (`liftedFieldText()`, half a point at 2x, a point at 1x), since AppKit's text field sets its line about half a point lower than a SwiftUI label, which on a 1x screen put numbers a pixel below centre; units (px, %, °, s) are a fixed `UnitSuffix` at the end of a field, never part of the typed text (typing one is still accepted); the field being typed in has a thin accent ring (`focusRing`); a field's name never shrinks.
- **Colour picking** is the app's own `ColorPicker` (saturation square, hue and alpha bars, eyedropper, hex) on a `PopupCard`, raised by `ColorWell` through `MenuHost.showPopup`. Under the hex are the document colours (30 September 2026): `Project.documentColors(limit:)` (MotionModel, tested: every solid fill, gradient stop, stroke, text colour and shadow, the colours actions change to and the background, one per step of 255, most used first, at most 16), handed to the well through `\.documentColors` (set in `EditorView`) and read when the card opens. The card is drawn once, so `color` stays what it was when it opened: `ColorPicker.picked` holds what was last taken here (a swatch, the eyedropper, the hex, a drag), or the square and hex kept the old colour. No third-party code: Flip vendors a library for this; Twine does not. `HexField` takes Figma's short forms on commit (`RGBA(typed:)`: `0` is `000000`, `1a` is `1A1A1A`, `abc` is `AABBCC`), and a click selects its digits through `ClickToType`, an AppKit click catcher laid over it while it is not being typed in, as `ScrubSurface` is over number fields.
