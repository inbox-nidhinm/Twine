# The document and its rules

Decisions worth knowing about the document and its rules, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` on 28 October 2026 so they load only when needed. Keep each entry up to date when its code changes.

- **Undo is by snapshot.** `ProjectStore` records the whole `Project` before each command. Drags and sliders use `beginContinuousChange` / `updateContinuousChange` / `endContinuousChange` so they undo as one step. History is capped (28 September 2026): at most `ProjectStore.undoSteps` (200) steps, and about `undoBudget` (512 MB) of snapshots, each step's cost estimated when recorded (`cost(of:against:)`: the layer and action lists it copied; paths and words inside layers are not counted); past the budget the oldest steps go, by lowering the undo manager's `levelsOfUndo` for a moment. `undoCosts`/`redoCosts` follow undo and redo. `perform(_:joining:)` adds to the last step when it came from there with the same key and nothing has happened since (any other step, undo, redo or a continuous change clears `joinable`): typing uses it, so a change made between letters is its own step in order, rather than one change held open swallowing it.
- **Skew and tilt** (28 September 2026): `Transform.skew` (degrees each way: x slants the sides, the bottom moving right; y the top and bottom) and `Transform.tilt` (`Tilt`: x tips around a level line through the anchor, top away; y turns around an upright one, right side away; `depth` is the viewing distance, 1200 by default), both left out of files when zero. A layer's flat transform is `Affine.layer(... skew:)` (scale, skew, rotation about the anchor), and every place that rebuilds one passes the skew, so resizing, anchors and grouping stay exact for skewed layers (ungrouping a skewed group loses its skew, as it loses an uneven stretch). A tilt is not flat: `Projective` (a 3 by 3 perspective matrix, exact for a flat layer turned in depth, which `caTransform` hands to Core Animation, so canvas, exports and thumbnails match; `WarpRenderingTests` checks pixels) with `Projective.layer` tilting after rotation. `ResolvedLayer.transform` stays flat (editing works in it); `.projection` and `ResolvedScene.worldProjection` are as drawn, and `worldCorners` (outlines, bounds, snapping), hit testing, clipping checks and `ResolvedMask.transform` follow them. A tilted layer, or anything in a tilted group, shows its outline but no handles (`CanvasNSView.isTilted`), since the handles' maths are flat; it is sized in the inspector. Dragging layers inside a tilted group is flat, so a little off. `Warp` boxes skew and tilt in `LayerState` and `ResolvedLayer` (an `indirect` case, `.none` for straight layers), as picture paints are, to keep states small. Skew and Tilt actions (`SkewAction`, `TiltAction`, To or By, the `skew` and `tilt` channels; By adds a tilt's angles and takes its depth) are offered wherever Move is. In the inspector they are Skew and Tilt entries in the Effects section (added from its + menu, shown while set; removing one zeroes it), Tilt with Depth once tilted.
- **The action rules live in one place**: `walk` in `MotionEvaluation/ActionTracks.swift`, with its numbered comment. Each action drives one channel (position, scale, rotation, opacity, size, visibility). On one channel of one layer, actions go in start order (ties by `rowOrder`); an empty initial value inherits the value at the action's start; the first action's explicit initial value holds from time 0 (entrances); a later action takes over from the value at its own start. Channels never interfere. By mode adds (Scale By multiplies); To is absolute. Resize grows around the anchor. A layer is shown until something hides it: a Show never hides what comes before it (the user's choice on 28 September 2026; it used to hide the layer until it when it was the first). Show and Hide are kept whatever else the layer does (rule 10, the user's choice on 28 September 2026, as in Jitter): a Show or Hide at or before a channel's first action stops that action's starting value holding from 0 s, so a layer shown at 1 s with a Fade In at 3 s shows as designed until 3 s; a Show brings back a layer faded right out (opacity only, to its design; `withShows`, a `revive` step), letters faded out by a text preset too; an In preset (`PresetKind.entering`, now in MotionModel) counts as a Show on the visibility track of a layer that has a Show or Hide, so one after a Hide plays.
- **Easing** is a named preset with an intensity or a custom bezier. The presets are Jitter's (28 September 2026): Smooth, Natural, Slow down, Accelerate, Elastic (stored as `spring`), Bounce, Overshoot, Impulse, Swing and Linear (stored as `none`), plus the user's Ease out back (0.45, 1.45, 0.8, 1) and Ease in back (0.3, -0.05, 0.7, -0.5), added 4 October 2026: beziers that leave 0...1, so past the standard intensity they stray further from the line, as the lists do, rather than being applied twice (which would clip them). Presets are worked out at evaluation, so improving one improves every action. At the standard intensity (0.5) each is exactly the designer's curve: the four beziers in `designCurve`, the rest CSS `linear()` lists of 101 points in `EasingTables` (MotionEvaluation). Lower intensity fades a preset towards a straight line (a bezier by drawing its handles in); higher strengthens it (a bezier towards itself applied twice, a list by doubling how far it strays from the line, Bounce by deepening its bounces after it first lands). `Spring` is no longer used by a preset.
- **Actions follow their layers**: `EditorCommand.apply` drops actions whose layer is gone, whatever command removed it. A copy is the layer whole under a new id (`Layer.withID`; copying field by field once lost blend modes and the proportions link), with new ids for its fills, strokes and effects. A mask copies as a mask. Carrying a layer through a flipped group (a negative scale, `Arrangement.mirrors`) turns its rotation and skew the other way, and its actions' (`remapped(mirrored:)`). Times are held to `Composition.longest` (a day) by the commands and the Duration field, and frame numbers are made whole through `FrameRate.whole`, so no typed time can crash; a damaged file's frame rate of 0 reads as 30. Grouping and ungrouping remap To values and initial values into the new parent space (`Action.remapped`); duplicating and pasting copy actions through `duplicated(recordingIDs:)` and `actionCopies(for:)`.
- **File format 2** is the first with actions. Anything older is refused with a plain message (`ProjectFileError.olderVersion`, whose `failureReason` is what the open alert shows). **Format 6** (17 October 2026) added frames in 3D and Z and changes nothing older. **Format 10** (19 October 2026) added Glass and changes nothing older. **Format 4** (4 October 2026) added angular gradients and changes nothing older; **format 5** (the same day) made masks Figma's, older masks read into `Layer.legacyMask` and converted as the project opens. **Format 3** made pictures fills: `PictureMigration` (in `FileFormat.swift`, on the raw JSON) turns each old picture layer into a rectangle with an image fill, keeping its id, so actions and masks carry on; one stretched out of its picture's shape becomes a Crop of the whole box, so it looks the same. Opening an old file does not rewrite it until it is edited.
- **Files from anyone** (4 October 2026, after a security review): `ProjectFormat.decode`, which the editor, `Creations.read` and the Info card share, brings every JSON number past `FileSafety.limit` (1e9) back to it before anything reads the file (`FileSafety.numbersHeld`, a byte scan that copies nothing when all is in range, about 8 ms of the 84 ms the 9 MB large project takes to decode), then `Project.heldInRange()` clamps the composition (1 to 16,384 px, at most `longest`), action times and motion blur, media lengths, type sizes (`FileSafety.fontSizes`) and polygon and star corners (`FileSafety.corners`, 3 to 60, which `ShapePaths` applies too), and gives a media asset whose `fileName` is not plain (`FileSafety.isPlainName`: no path, not hidden) its own id as a name, so it shows grey. Every read of a package's file goes through `ProjectDocument.packageFile`, which refuses names that are not plain and anything not an ordinary file (a link could lead anywhere); `read(from:)` drops such entries from the media folder so no save carries them on. Where a number becomes a count or a picture's pixels, use `Int(saturating:)`: `Int(_:)` on a Double past `Int.max` stops the app (a Volume fade a billion years long did, on open). `HugeValueTests` and `FileSafetyTests` cover it. Figma pastes (`FigmaImport.read`) and Twine's own clipboards (`LayerClipboard.read`, `ActionClipboard.read`) go through `numbersHeld` and `FileSafety.held` too, and pictures inside an SVG or a paste must pass `FileSafety.pictureFits` (16,384 a side, 100 million pixels). For SVGs (4 October 2026): `SVGImport.utf8` turns UTF-16 and UTF-32 into UTF-8 before the byte checks and refuses any other encoding that does not write letters as themselves (a UTF-16 file's entities slipped past `declaresRiskyEntities`); `pictureSVG` hands the system only the definitions a picture refers to (`references(in:)`, followed through), counts what it draws with every `use` expanded (`expandedSize`) against `maxElements`, and an `SVGImport.Budget` (shared by every SVG in one Figma paste) holds all pictures to `maxPictureWork` elements and `maxPicturePixels` (4 billion, about ten seconds in release), past which the file or paste is `tooComplex`; files over `largestFile` are not read. `HostileImportTests` covers it. `SVGPictures.draw` draws one picture at a time (a lock): the system's SVG drawing asks the font service for each text's font, and the test suite's parallel SVG tests asking at once left every thread waiting on it for good, three times on 4 October 2026. Picture files (4 October 2026): `MediaLibrary.loadImage` reads one past `pictureFits` shrunk to fit as it reads (`FileSafety.pictureDrawingScale`, through ImageIO's thumbnail, which does not hold the whole picture: a 900-megapixel PNG took 4 s and 1.5 GB), and does not read one past `pictureFileFits` (400 million pixels, 65,535 a side) at all; imports refuse those with a notice (`MediaImport.isOversizedPicture`, `oversizedNotice`). `LargePictureTests` covers it. WebM files must start with Matroska's header and are read by ffmpeg and ffprobe with `-f matroska -protocol_whitelist file` (`WebMConversion.readAsWebM`): left to judge by content, ffmpeg read a `.webm` that was an ffconcat list as the files it named.
- **Paths are plain data** (`BezierPath`), for SVG import, boolean operations and morphing later. A pen path (`.path` geometry) fills its layer's box like every other shape: `BezierPath.fitted(from: bounds, to: size)` stretches the stored points' curve bounds (`BezierPath.bounds`, true extrema, not handles) onto the box, in `resolve` and in `ShapeGeometry.bezierPath(size:)`. Editing points keeps the stored bounds equal to the box (`PathEditing.reshaped`), so the stretch is only ever non-trivial after a resize.
