# Frames in 3D and templates

Decisions worth knowing about frames in 3D and templates, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` on 28 October 2026 so they load only when needed. Keep each entry up to date when its code changes.

- **Frames in 3D** (17 October 2026, templates milestones 1 and 2): `FrameStyle.space` (`Space3D`: camera `distance`, `isIsometric`, `vanishingPoint` as a share of the box, `depthFade`, `depthBlur`, `backOpacity`; only what differs from a new one is written, so `{}` means on; file format 6) holds a frame's own children in one space; `Transform.z` (left out at 0) is towards the viewer; outside a 3D frame (since milestone 4) a layer with a Z or a bend is placed through its own camera (`Camera3D.own`: over its anchor, at its tilt's depth, `SpaceContext(ownOf:)`) with depth 0, so it keeps its siblings' order, as do a text's letters (`Camera3D.ownProjection`) and motion blur's copies; `Layer.space3D` and `Project.space3D(holding:)` read it. The maths is `MotionEvaluation/Camera3D.swift`: `Pose3D.layer` places a layer as `Projective.layer` does (flat part, then the tilt on y and x about the anchor or a hinge) at its Z, and `Camera3D.projection(of:)` turns the pose into the layer's exact `Projective` (a layer's own tilt equals a camera over its anchor at the tilt's depth, `aTiltIsTheSameTurnSeenFromAboveItsAnchor`; in 3D the tilt's depth is unused, so the inspector hides it). `evaluate`'s `walk` takes the `SpaceContext` of the frame whose children it walks and `place`s each child after resolving it: `ResolvedLayer.placed` (`Placed3D`, boxed) replaces its `projection` and carries its middle's depth; behind the frame's plane it fades and blurs, reaching the full amount a frame's longer side back; with its back to the camera (`seesFront`) it takes `backOpacity`; once a corner nears the camera (divide under 0.05) it is not drawn. Placement is applied outside the cache, and a still group's whole run is not reused inside a space (its place depends on the frame), so a changed camera always shows (`theCacheKeepsUpWithTheFrame`). `WorldTransforms` places masks the same way, and motion blur's copies go through the camera. Only direct children are placed: a group or frame inside is a flat card. The renderer sets each container's `zPosition` from the depth: Core Animation draws siblings in that order, keeps equal ones in list order (`layersAtTheSameDepthKeepTheirOrder`) and, as no depth reaches the divide, moves nothing. `ResolvedScene.drawingOrder()` is that order, for hit testing (`layer(at:deep:)` goes front to back by it). On the canvas a placed layer counts as tilted (`isTilted`): outline, no handles; dragging is flat, so a little off in perspective; ungrouping or moving a layer out of a 3D frame keeps its Z, unused. The inspector's `SpaceSection` (frames, after Layout; no vanishing point when isometric) and a Z row in Position (inside a 3D frame). Z is animated by the Depth action (`DepthAction`, To or By, the `depth` channel, a moving channel for motion blur; offered after Tilt wherever Move is): Z lives in `Warp` beside skew and tilt (`LayerState.z`, `ResolvedLayer.warp.z`, which `place` reads), so most layers stay a pointer. A 3D frame's camera is animated by the Camera action (`CameraAction`, a whole `Space3D` start and end, the `camera` channel, category `.space`, titled 3D): `LayerState.camera` (`CameraState`, boxed) starts as the design's space, `Space3D.interpolate` blends every number but keeps `isIsometric` from where it starts, and `walk` places a frame's children by its state's camera, `WorldTransforms` by the frame's state too. Copying a Depth onto another layer shifts it by the difference in Z (`ActionOrigin.z`, optional for older clipboards). **Bend** (`Transform.bend`, degrees of a turn across and down, positive bringing the middle forward; in `Warp` with skew, tilt and Z; the Bend action, `BendAction`, To or By, the `bend` channel, offered after Tilt wherever Move is; an Effects entry as Skew and Tilt are): `Curve` moves a layer's points off its plane, `SpaceContext.place` projects a grid of them (`BentMesh`, 16 cells across a bent way and 4 along a straight one) as `Placed3D.bent`, whose projection is the one through its four corners (`Projective.box`, for outlines and clicks), and drops its motion blur. A bent layer outside a 3D frame is seen from its own tilt's camera (`SpaceContext(ownOf:)`) and keeps depth 0, so it stays in its siblings' order. The renderer draws it with Core Animation's private `CAMeshTransform` (`MeshWarp`, set as `meshTransform` on the container, whose own transform is then the identity; a layer's own clip comes after its mesh, so frames clip one layer in, in their children's holder, which is why a bent frame's rounded clip bends; `aBentFrameCurvesEverythingInIt`), chosen in the spike over strips, which seam and bend only pictures; without the class a bent layer draws flat through its corners. Seen nearly edge-on, a bent layer wraps round its own outline and its projected mesh folds (cells facing both ways; each cell is convex on its own, so check facing, not convexity). With every vertex at depth 0 the window server drew a folded mesh as a large slab (CARenderer, so exports, drew it right); `BentMesh.depths` carries each point's depth and `MeshWarp` hands it over in widths (`width` normalisation), so the screen draws the near side over the far (`aCurvedCardSeenEdgeOnStaysCurved`, 18 October 2026). A bent layer (not a group) partly turned away, with the space's backs dimmed, has two faces (19 October 2026, the user's request: the whole card dimming as its middle passed edge-on jumped): `place` keeps the cells facing the camera on the layer (`BentMesh.side` `.front`, `facesFront` by each cell's turn as drawn) and returns its back, a layer of its own (`BentMesh.backID`, `side` `.back`, `owner`, `Placed3D.backOf`) at the backs' strength, which `evaluate` puts before it; each is ordered by its own cells' mean depth, so the farther face is under. `MeshWarp` draws only a side's faces. The canvas takes a click on a back for its layer (`backOf`). Tests that count a scene's cards leave backs out. Drawing folded cards flat instead was tried and dropped: the switch between flat and curved jumped visibly. A mask that bends is placed flat, approximately. **Orbit** (`Space3D.orbit`): the whole space turned about the frame's middle (y, then x) before the camera, blended by the Camera action, so a ring spins with one action. `Space3DRenderingTests` checks pixels; a test process cannot show a window, so screen checks save the layer tree (`TWINE_SPIKE_OUT`) for `Tools/Spike3D/show-on-screen.swift`, and with it a `Ring.twine` the kit can open.
- **Templates** (17 October 2026, milestone 5): a template is a frame with `GroupContent.template` (`TemplateSetup`, MotionModel `Template.swift`: `TemplateKind`, `settings` by key as `TemplateValue`s, `CardRatio`, corner radius as a share of the card's shorter side, shadow, `pose`; file format 7). **Its slots are its children** (19 October 2026, the user's request, file format 9; built on branch `template-slot-layers`, merged the same day): each a picture layer, a video layer (muted) or an empty one (`TemplateSlots.layer`, MotionEditing; `TemplateSlots.emptyName`), read in slot order by `Layer.templateSlotLayers` (children reversed, so slot 1 is the top row of the layers panel and dragging rows reorders slots) and `templateSlots` (each one's `slotMedia`); never drawn, played or animated themselves (`evaluate` walks the cards, not the children; `Project.templateSlotIDs` keeps them from `MediaPlays.withPlayBars`, `ActionFactory.kinds` and canvas drags). Older files' slot lists are read into `TemplateSetup.legacySlots` and made children as the project opens (`TemplateSlots.converted`, before `withPlayBars`). A selected slot shows `TemplateSlotSection` in place of the layer inspector in both modes (preview, Choose or Replace, Empty Slot, drops; `EditorController.templateSlot`, `emptyTemplateSlot`, `addTemplateSlot`, `setTemplateMedia` keeping a refilled slot's id); the template's own section no longer lists slots. A double-click on a card goes in to its slot (`TemplateCards.slot(of:in:setup:phase:)`, `EditorController.templateSlotLayer(showing:in:)`); the kit's `tcard X Y` logs it. Its cards are made each moment by `TemplateCards.make` from its recipe (`TemplateRecipes`, MotionEvaluation: `controls(kind)`, each a number with a range or a choice, read through `Settings`, which holds every number to its range and so caps cards for files from anywhere; `scene(setup, phase:, size:)` gives a `Space3D` and `TemplateCard`s, a cell, place, Z, turn, tilt, the radii a card's curve follows, scale and opacity; streams fade cards out at their ends so they go round unseen) as rounded rectangles filled with their slots' pictures (going round the slots again), fitted to their cells, with ids worked out from the frame's and the card's place (`TemplateCards.id`, `isCard`) so the renderer keeps each card. `walk` in `evaluate` walks those cards in the recipe's camera at the loop's `phase`: the template bar (`ActionSpec.template`, `TemplatePlay.loops`, the `template` channel, `LayerState.template`, a boxed `TemplateState`) adds its loops to the pose, so the bar ends where it began, and stretching it slows it. `Templates` (MotionEditing): the `library` with Twine's own names in `TemplateGroup`s, `frame(_:filling:pictures:)`, `adding` (one undo step: the frame in front, filling the composition, the bar across the scene, the selected picture layers taken into the slots and removed), and `split` (Split into Layers: real cards at the pose, and across the bar `splitSteps` (24) a loop of To actions, Move, Depth, Tilt, Rotate, Scale, Bend, Opacity and Camera, only where something changes, a card hidden at both ends of a step going at once, all in a `TimelineScene` named after the template; `splittingMakesRealCardsThatPlayTheSame`). App: `TemplatePicker` (tiles drawn by `TemplatePosters` through an `OffscreenRenderer`, stills cached, the hovered one played on a `TimelineView`), `AnimationInspector`'s Add to Layer section (`ActionTiles`: New Action and Select Preset, each half the row, as the Templates tile is, so every tile is one size) and under it a Templates section (`TemplateTile`), which shows alone when nothing is selected (19 October 2026, the user's layout), `TemplateSection` in Design in place of the 3D section, `EditorController.addTemplate`, `splitTemplate`, `editTemplate`, `chooseTemplatePicture`; a click on a card picks its template (`layer(at:deep:)`). The kit's `template KIND`, `tset`, `tsplit` and `render NAME` steps (the last draws the composition offscreen, which works while the Mac is locked, when window shots fail). An empty slot shows one of the app's ten sample pictures (`TemplateSamples`, MotionModel: fixed ids, file names and sizes, round the samples by slot; `template-sample-NN.jpg` in `Twine/Resources/TemplateSamples`, the user's illustrations sized to at most 1024 a side, 19 October 2026), never a project's media: `Project.pictureSizes` includes them, `TemplateSampleFiles` (app) finds them in the bundle for the editor's `MediaLibrary` (first, then the document) and the posters' renderers, and `releaseUnusedMedia` always keeps them. A drop on a card showing a sample fills the first empty slot showing that sample. The flat groups (`FlatTemplateRecipes.swift`, milestone 6) lean on `TemplateCard.area` (a piece of a bigger picture: the picture covers the area, given in the card's own pixels, and the card, exactly its cell, shows its part, drawn as an `ImagePaint` crop), `slot` (which picture), `color` (a plain card: a dimming veil, a wipe's glowing edge) and `rounded`; wipes step from picture to picture, an angled edge drawn as 48 level strips each reaching as far as the edge does at its height, and flips are two cards back to back in a space whose backs are hidden. `TemplateRecipes.restingPose` is where a new one rests (past any building up, so Design and the picker's still are not empty). The picker draws its tiles in a `.task` after the view is built, never in `body`: drawing flushes Core Animation, which rebuilt the tile mid-way and stopped the app (a `dispatch_once` trap). They are drawn on `TemplatePosters`' own queue, not the main thread: drawn there while the picker was opening, the first ten came out blank in release builds and were kept (18 October 2026); a still with nothing drawn (`isBlank`) is drawn again, never kept. `TemplateTests` judges a loop's seam by the largest change between two moments anywhere else in it, since stepping templates jump at every step. The Sequences (`SequenceTemplateRecipes.swift`, milestone 7, `TemplateGroup.sequence`) are lists of `Shot`s played round by `play`: each `Look` is cards, a space, and a camera `focus` and `zoom` (applied by `seen`, which grows everything about the focus and draws the camera back as much, so the picture is exact); a shot rests for the Hold's share of its length, then each card moves to the next shot's place at its own time (`rank` times Stagger), eased, turning over on the way when Flip is set. Every card keeps one 4:3 cell (`Deck`) and is sized by its scale, so Split into Layers, which writes no sizes, keeps it; `Templates.splitSteps(for:)` gives sequences 48 steps a loop, and `split` unwinds turns (`nearest`) so a card going round never turns back the long way. **Template Setting** (`ActionSpec.templateSetting`, `TemplateSettingAction`: a `key`, an optional initial value and an end, in the inspector's units; file format 8; the `templateSetting` channel, offered only on template frames): `TemplateState` carries `settings` beside the loops, every animatable setting from the design (`TemplateRecipes.designSettings`, `animatable`: the recipe's numbers and `TemplateSettingAction.cornerRadius`), and `walk` walks each key's actions as a track of its own; `evaluate` makes the cards from `setup.tuned(settings)`, which `Settings` holds to each range. `split` works out every step's cards first (settings at each step through `ActionTracks`), makes every card that ever shows (hidden where it is not), adds Resize and Corner Radius where they change, and deletes the bar and the setting actions. **New from Template** (`Templates.project`, `length(of:)`): Home's button raises `TemplateGalleryCard` (`Library.pickingTemplate`; laid out from the user's mock on 19 October 2026: two floating panels on the app's grained ground, as onboarding has, `Theme.templateGalleryWidthShare` and `HeightShare` of the window (75% and 80%, the user's choice); the groups as Home's sidebar rows, the chosen one on `panelRaised`; the group's tiles as many across as fit at `templateTileWidth` or wider, under its name in the display face; each tile's picture is drawn square on no ground at its size, `TemplatePosters.Style.gallery(side:)`, rounded up to a step, inset as its name is; `defaultScrollAnchor(.top)`, as the grid otherwise opened part way down), and `Library.newProject(from:)` writes the project through `Creations.makeProject(named:in:project:)`. The kit's `templategallery` and `newfromtemplate KIND` steps drive it. **Videos in slots**: `TemplateCards.make(videos:)` (`Project.videoClips`, `VideoClip`: size and length, worked out by `evaluate` only once a template asks) makes a card showing a video a frame with the card's radius, clipping, holding the video (`TemplateCards.videoID(of:)`, muted, fit Fill; for an `area` piece placed at the area it covers), and `evaluate` sets each such video's `sourceTime` to the scene's time going round the clip (`videoTime`); its sound is never heard. The canvas's hit test takes a click on the video for its card's template (`templateCard(of:in:)`). `split` gives each video Play bars back to back from 0 to the scene's end. **Dropping files**: `EditorController.setTemplateMedia(_:from:urls:)` brings in pictures and videos (`slotKind`) into slots from one on, adding slots past the end, as one undo step; the Template section's wells and the slots grid take drops (`dropDestination`), and the canvas's `performDragOperation` sends files dropped on a card to `dropOnTemplate`, which picks the slot that card's picture or video is in, or the first empty one. The kit's `tslot N PATH`, `tdrop X Y PATH` and `tslots` steps drive them; its `render` step now draws with the project's media.
