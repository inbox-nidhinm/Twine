# Glass: handoff notes

19 to 21 October 2026, v0.2.35 to v0.2.43. Figma's Glass effect, built in Twine as an effect and an action. These notes are for whoever carries the work on: what is built, how it works, every problem met on the way and why it happened, what is still open, and how to test it. The short version of the design is in `CLAUDE.md` under **Glass**; this is the long one.

## What the user asked for

"Support for liquid glass in effects, the same or similar to how Figma has implemented it", with a Glass action to animate it. Later, from the user: Depth is limited to 100 px. On 21 October 2026, after the screen kept showing through: "do the rebuild, draw behind the glass yourself", and after a review of the whole effect, "fix all of them".

## What is built

- **The effect** (`EffectKind.glass(Glass)`, file format 10): closed shapes and frames only, the first one switched on counting, as with background blur. Open pen paths and lines are not offered it (`ShapeGeometry.isClosed`, in `ActionFactory` and the inspector's Effects menu). Figma's panel settings, in Figma's order: Light (angle on Figma's dial, 0 at the top and -45 the top left, plus intensity), Refraction, Depth (0 to 100 px), Dispersion, Frost (px of blur, Figma's blur units) and Splay. `Glass()` is Figma's new glass (-45°, 80%, 80, 20, 50, 4, 0); `Glass.noGlass` is none; `Glass.held` clamps a file's numbers.
- **The inspector**: Effects + menu > Glass. `GlassControls` (`Twine/Components/PaintControls.swift`) draws the settings for both the effect and the action. The app says `Glass` through a typealias to MotionModel's, because SwiftUI on macOS 26 has a `Glass` of its own and the names clash.
- **The action** (`GlassAction`, the `glass` channel, listed under Blur): every setting blends, the light turning the shorter way round. From no glass it brings Figma's new glass in; on a layer with glass it takes it away. A setting that shows nothing at one end takes the other end's (`Glass.interpolate`): a light that is off has no angle and glass that bends nothing no depth or splay, so fading glass in or out only fades it (`fadingGlassKeepsItsLight`). The swap button works.
- **Figma**: the plugin copies `GLASS` effects (`FigmaPlugin/code.js`), and `FigmaImport.effect` reads them. Figma's frost is the effect's `radius`. Figma's plugin API has no Splay yet, so pasted glass has Splay 0.
- **The AI tools**: `ProjectOutline` names the effect `glass`; the action list comes from `ActionFactory`, so it is already there.

## How it draws

Everywhere (the canvas, the export preview, exports, thumbnails, stills, the AI tools' `render_frame`), Twine draws a picture of the glass over the layers behind it with backdrops off screen, and shows that picture. `CoreAnimationRenderer.drawsGlassBehind` switches it on: `CanvasNSView`, `ExportPreviewView` and `OffscreenRenderer` all do. The renderer that draws the picture uses backdrops directly, as exports did until 21 October 2026.

### The glass itself (backdrops)

All in `Packages/MotionKit/Sources/MotionRendering/Glass.swift`, whose header comment holds the spike's findings. It uses the private pieces macOS 26 builds its own Liquid Glass from. Twine is internal only, so private APIs are fine.

1. **Backdrops.** A `CABackdropLayer` shows what is behind the layer through Core Animation's `displacementMap` filter (Refraction). Over it, a second backdrop shows that through `chromaticAberrationMap` (Dispersion) and then `gaussianBlur` (Frost). It has to be two backdrops. A map filter that comes after another filter on the same backdrop reads its map out of place: the colours split in a cyan band along the top and bottom edges, and the bend disappeared.
2. **The field.** Each backdrop has a sublayer named `glassField`: a `CASDFLayer` (a distance field of the outline) holding one `CASDFElementLayer`, run through a `CASDFGlassDisplacementEffect`. Its `height` is Depth, and its `curvature` is 1 + 3 × Splay. A field that no filter reads draws itself as a blue card, and a hidden field bends nothing, so each is shown exactly while a filter reads it.
3. **The outline.** A box with every corner rounded alike (`ResolvedShape.roundedBox`: uniform rectangles, and circles filling a square box) uses the element's own `bounds` mode with a `cornerRadius`. It is exact and costs nothing. Every other outline (stars, ovals, polygons, pen paths, uneven corners) is a picture the element reads in its `contents` mode (`GlassField.picture`):
   - red is the signed distance, mapped between `contentsZeroValueDistance` and `contentsOneValueDistance`;
   - green and blue are the way out to the nearest edge, 0.5 plus half the unit direction (with green and blue at 0.5 nothing bends; with them at 0 the bend leans to one side);
   - it must be 16 bit and tagged sRGB, or the values are colour converted and the edge moves inward;
   - it is worked out at the pixels per point it shows at (zoom included, rounded up to a power of two, at most 2048 texels a side), 20 to 50 ms in release. At one texel per point a small star zoomed in bent in blotches.
4. **The light.** Over the fills, two more distance-field layers with `CASDFGlassHighlightEffect` light the rim from the angle, and from the opposite side at 60%. Each reaches 120° round the rim, about 3 px wide at depth 20. They are the layer's own, so they stay sharp on the canvas; checked on a dark ground to fall on the same side on the canvas as in an export.
5. **How deep it bends.** `GlassKit.fitted` holds Depth to the outline's own thickness, how far in its deepest point lies: half the box's shorter side for a rounded box, and for anything else `GlassField.thickness` (measured on a 48 by 48 grid, cached per outline). Deeper, the bend reaches across the shape and turns its middle inside out (`thinShapesNeverTurnInsideOut`).
6. **How far it reaches.** `GlassKit.margin` (how far past the box the backdrops, fields and elements reach) is the depth, the frost's reach (1.5 times the frost, three of its Gaussian's deviations, at most `frostReach`, 600), and 8 more. The field only exists inside its element's frame, so it must reach past the shape; cut off at the edge, the bend went one-sided.

Strength constants are in `GlassKit` and were picked by eye, not matched to Figma: `refractionPerDepth` 1.0 (pixels the edge moves things, per pixel of depth, at full refraction), `dispersionPerDepth` 0.5, `oppositeLight` 0.6, `lightSpread` 120°.

### The picture

`CoreAnimationRenderer.drawGlassBehind`, run by `render` after its own transaction (and again when `visibleArea` changes), for each glass layer:

1. **What is behind** is every layer drawn before the glass in the screen's own order (`ResolvedScene.drawingOrder()`: each group before its children, and in a 3D frame its children by depth, so a card nearer the camera is never behind, `glassSeesOnlyWhatIsBehindItInDepth`), kept in list order. The groups holding the glass are drawn at full strength (`atFullStrength`: no fade, blend, blur, mask, reveal, curl, motion copies or frame strokes), since whatever they do to the glass they do to its picture on the canvas; drawn in the picture too, it came out twice, or, through a backdrop in a faded group (drawn on its own), saw nothing behind it and came out white (`glassInAFadedGroupStillSeesWhatIsBehind`). Last comes `glassOnly(layer)`: the glass layer with no fills, strokes, shadows, blurs, mask, reveal, curl, motion blur, fade or blend, and its light off. Time is set to 0, so a still background is not drawn again.
2. **Drawn** by a `GlassBehind` (`GlassBehind.swift`, one per glass layer): its own `CoreAnimationRenderer` (backdrops) through a `CARenderer` into `IOSurface`-backed Metal textures. It draws into one the screen is not using (not the one handed over last, nor one `isInUse`), making a third or fourth when all are held, so two redraws in one frame never draw into the picture on screen. The same scene, transform and size as last time draws nothing.
3. **Upright, over the composition.** The picture covers the box round the glass's working area (`GlassLayers.whole`, through `worldProjection`), clipped to the composition and, on screen, to what the canvas shows (`visibleArea`) and as far again as the glass reaches, snapped out to quarters of the view so a small pan draws nothing again (`glassDrawsOnlyWhatTheCanvasShowsAndLooksTheSame`). Snapped to whole pixels, at the canvas's pixels a point (`Node.pixelScale`: the window's backing scale times the zoom, up to 8), at most `GlassBehind.largestSide` (4096) a side. Upright, so its pixels meet the screen's: drawn in the layer's own turned points, a turned card's stripes came out soft.
4. **Shown** by `GlassLayers.show(_:area:toLocal:)`: the glass is one plain holder (no filters, no backdrop) under the fills, clipped to the outline a pixel wide (`mask.lineWidth`, so the picture's own smoothed edge is not smoothed again), holding the picture placed through the inverse of the layer's world projection (turned over off screen, as other pictures).

So the glass can only ever show the composition, the same at every zoom and up to every edge of the window, and the canvas is the export.

**Traps met building it:**

- **Never flush Core Animation on the calling thread.** The canvas renders from SwiftUI's `updateNSView` (flushing there once stopped the app with a `dispatch_once` trap in the template picker) and redraws glass from `layoutWorld`, inside an explicit transaction, where a flush commits nothing. `GlassBehind.draw` runs its work, flush included, with `async` on its own queue and waits on a semaphore. Not `sync`: it runs the work on the calling thread, and a pan's redraw, inside the layout transaction, drew the picture at its old size, magnified, the rest black (v0.2.42 had `sync`; `aPictureOfWhatIsBehindRedrawnLargerIsWhole` draws inside a transaction).
- **`render` must not be called inside an explicit `CATransaction`** of the caller's with glass to draw, or the tree it draws from is not committed. `OffscreenRenderer.draw` sets its scale and transform in a transaction of its own and calls `render` outside it.
- **Filtering the picture again on the canvas does not work.** A bend holder inside a colour split's holder read the split's map out of place (the old cyan band) and turned cards came out wrong; clipping the inner holder to the outline made the frost and split blur in nothing from past the edge. Hence the picture carries the finished glass.
- **Turning a picture over off screen** with a −1 vertical scale needs the turn about its middle, or it swings out of view (the first drawn glass showed nothing at all). The placement folds the turn into one transform.
- **A zoom must draw again** (v0.2.42, the user's report: "zooming in and out and clicking on the object changes the view"). Zooming changes the canvas's pixels a point (`contentsScale`) but nothing in the project, so nothing called `render`, and the glass showed its picture from before the zoom, stretched and soft, until a click drew the scene again. `CanvasNSView.updateContentsScale` (zoom, fit, a move to another screen) and the export preview's `place` draw once more a turn later (`redrawSoon`), so a pinch draws at most once a frame and never inside a SwiftUI update. A pan changes `visibleArea`, which redraws the glass alone. The kit check: zoom in steps with no playing between, pan, then `selectlayer`; the circle's pixels must not change but for the selection's outline, and must match the export (`render`). `glassLooksTheSameAtEveryScale` checks the bend and light reach as far at 1 and 2 pixels a point.
- **An `IOSurface`'s rows must be whole steps of 16 bytes for Metal.** A 2,254 pixel picture (the user's circle at 4×) stopped the app; rows are rounded up to 64 bytes (`aPictureOfWhatIsBehindTakesAnyWidth`).

## Issues met, and how each was fixed

1. **Distance pictures read wrongly** (the spike, 19 October 2026). Uniform values were colour converted (fixed by tagging sRGB); the distance was read from red, with green and blue as the direction (found by trial); and pictures run upside down off screen, so the rows and the vertical direction flip when `flipsContents` is on. The light's angle needs no flip.
2. **The butterfly** (v0.2.37, v0.2.43). The user dragged Depth to 639 px on a 248 px circle. The bend then pulled in picture from hundreds of points away, turning the middle inside out into two mirrored lobes. Fixed by `GlassKit.fitted` and the 100 px cap, but a star's points, far thinner than its box, still scrambled its middle at Depth 100 until depth was held to the outline's own thickness (v0.2.43).
3. **The screen showing through the glass** (v0.2.36 to v0.2.41). The user saw the tab bar, the Dock and the menu bar inside the glass, live, and worst when zoomed in.
   - **Why:** on screen, a backdrop shows whatever the window server has on the screen where it lies, other windows and the Dock included. Glass bends in picture from just past its edge, and zooming in scales that reach (Depth 100 at 4× reaches 400 points), so glass near the window's edge pulled in what was past the window.
   - **Tried, and not enough:** making the canvas its own group (a no-op `colorMatrix` filter, an explicit identity matrix, opacity 0.999 with group opacity, rasterising: a backdrop ignores them all; v0.2.36, taken out in v0.2.37). Cutting what each backdrop looks at to the part of the composition the canvas shows (v0.2.38): right at the bottom edge, but at the top, under the title bar, a backdrop still saw what was behind the window (Chrome's tab strip, measured green against the canvas's neutral #1E1E1E). Cutting below the title bar too (v0.2.40) stopped that but left a hard line where the bend stopped, which the user saw as artefacts, and the user still saw the bottom of the screen in the glass. A standalone window with the same backdrops, two stages, a zoomed parent, a titled see-through title bar, the window key and opaque panels over it never leaked, so the cause was never pinned down; it lies in how Twine's window is put together.
   - **Fixed (v0.2.41)** by not looking at the screen at all: see "The picture" above.
   - **Learned on the way:** a backdrop cut short does not read the screen past its cut (a filtered backdrop cut at a window edge, with a magenta window just past it, showed no magenta). `CABackdropLayer` also has `backdropRect`, `marginWidth`, `bleedAmount`, `ignoresScreenClip` and `windowServerAware` (off by default), none needed.
4. **Blotchy bends when zoomed in** (v0.2.36): see the texel note in "How it draws" above.
5. **Rebuilding every frame while pinching** (v0.2.36). The canvas changes its pixel scale continuously during a pinch, so the field picture's scale is rounded to powers of two, and a rounded box (no picture) ignores the zoom altogether.
6. **Found in a review of the whole effect** (21 October 2026, all fixed in v0.2.43): thin shapes turned inside out (issue 2); glass in a faded group came out white in exports and twice faded on the canvas; Multiply on glass differed between canvas and export (now one path); a picture could be redrawn while on screen; the Glass action swung the light to -45° while fading glass out; open paths were offered Glass they could not draw; frost past about 43 px stopped blurring in what lay past 64 points; glass zoomed far in took up to 64 MB a picture (now no more than what the screen shows); in a 3D frame, a card nearer the camera but earlier in the list was bent into the glass.

## Still open

- **Not compared with Figma side by side.** The strengths (bend, colour split, rim width and brightness, how Splay spreads) were tuned by eye on stripes. The next step is a Figma frame with a few glass shapes at known settings, rendered in both apps and matched.
- **The light's angle from Figma is unverified.** The importer takes `lightAngle` exactly as Figma's plugin API gives it. If pasted glass is lit from the wrong side, convert it there.
- **Cost.** Each glass layer keeps a renderer of what is behind it and draws it again whenever anything behind changes; the user's scene plays at 59.8 fps, but many glass layers over a large project are unmeasured, as is a Resize on glass that is not a rounded box (its field picture is worked out again every frame, 3 to 50 ms).
- **Not supported:** glass on text (Figma may allow it), glass inside motion-blurred copies, and glass on a mask (mask nodes skip it).
- **Bent layers (Bend, `CAMeshTransform`) with glass** were never tried: the picture is placed through the layer's projection, which for a bent layer is only through its corners.
- **macOS 26 only.** Without the private classes, the glass draws as its frost alone. Without a GPU there is no glass at all.
- **Unused:** Core Animation's `glassBackground` and `glassForeground` filters exist, with many parameters, but did nothing useful in the spike. `CASDFGlassHighlightEffect` also has a `global` switch (light fixed to the screen, not the layer) that was not tried.

## How to test it

- **Unit tests:** `GlassRenderingTests` checks pixels through the export renderer: the bend stays near the edge, the colour split, frost, the light's side, non-box outlines, the field picture's values and its zoom, the depth cap and a thin shape's, the same at every scale; that the picture matches backdrops (bend, Figma's default, split, frost, a star, a turned card), never bends in a layer in front or one nearer in depth, sees behind a faded group, covers only what the canvas shows and looks the same, is whole when redrawn larger inside a transaction, and takes any width. `StyleActionTests.glassIsForShapesAndFramesAndBlendsEverySetting` and `fadingGlassKeepsItsLight` cover the blending. `TWINE_GLASS_OUT=folder swift test --filter GlassRenderingTests` writes each picture to look at.
- **Test projects:** `python3 Tools/SelfTest/make-glass-projects.py FOLDER` writes Glass, Mid, User and Edge (see its header), then run `Tools/SelfTest/run.sh FOLDER/User.twine NAME "steps"`. The kit has `zoom F`, `pan DX DY` and `render NAME` (the export renderer) steps for this. The test copy opens at the frame saved in its own defaults (`NSWindow Frame Main` in `com.nidhin.twine.selftest`); copy the user's from `com.nidhin.twine` to test their window exactly. A colour in a project's JSON is `{"type": "solid", "color": {...}}`: written wrong, the test copy shows an alert the user sees.
- **The canvas no longer depends on the screen**, so the kit's window shots show what the user sees. To prove it on the real screen, bring the test copy to the front and capture: glass across the window's top edge (`key m;zoom 2;zoom 2;wait 1;pan 0 -380` on User.twine) and its bottom (`pan 330 420`). `open -a build/TwineTest.app` brings the window over from another Space where `activate` does not; keep captures brief and say so first, since the user may be working.
- **Playback** (release test copy, `perfplay`, the user's scene of a Column Wall template behind a glass circle): 59.8 fps still, animating every frame through a Glass action, and at 4× zoom after a pan; 58.8 fps at 8×.
